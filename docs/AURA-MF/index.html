AURA-MPC

Model Predictive Control: Physics-Informed Surrogate for Photovoltaic Panel Orientation
Independent undergraduate research, Department of Physics, Engineering, and Astronomy, Austin Peay State University

Overview

AURA-MPC is a model predictive controller (MPC) for a 3-axis photovoltaic panel mount. Every 15 minutes the controller measures the weather, panel temperature, and pose, predicts the next 15 minutes for each candidate pose, picks the pose that maximizes electrical power, applies only that first move, and repeats. The forecasts come from a neural surrogate of a lumped thermal model, trained on synthetic data from an RK4 generator written in Fortran.

  • The surrogate matches its generator to 0.06 K at 15 minutes and retrains in about 2 minutes on a laptop CPU.
  • Under a power objective, the best pose is always the max-irradiance pose, so the surrogate's useful output is the 15-minute temperature forecast.
  • On a year of Sandia National Laboratories module data, a physics-calibrated model beats persistence (2.21 K vs 3.20 K RMSE). The zero-shot network does not.
  • In a closed-loop sandbox, the MPC captures 98.7% of oracle energy.

Model

Lumped energy balance (per unit area):

C dT/dt = αG − η(T) G − h(v) (T − Tamb)
η(T) = ηref [1 − β (T − TSTC)]
h(v) = h0 + k (v − 1 m/s)

Efficiency falls with temperature. h0 comes from NOCT and k from McAdams. Irradiance is recovered from the panel's short-circuit current, corrected for temperature.

Surrogates:

  • MLP: 8 inputs → steady-state temperature and 15-minute temperature, trained by MSE on 106 RK4 rows.
  • Physics-constrained model: the exact solution of the energy balance, T(t) = Tss + (T0 − Tss) e−κt/C. It learns only three physical constants (h0, k, C) plus a bounded residual.

MPC step: search a 19 × 19 pitch–yaw grid over a 15-minute horizon for the pose that maximizes G · η(T̂15).

Results

Temperature cannot move the pose

Differentiating P = Gη(T(G)) through the energy balance gives dP/dG > 0 for every irradiance below G*. The smallest G* over 300 sweep conditions is 4,013 W/m², while the largest irradiance available is 916 W/m² (model) and 1,485 W/m² (Sandia). The max-power pose matched the max-irradiance pose in 100% of conditions, so the MPC acts as a sun tracker.

  • The generator's wind slope is too steep: Sandia calibrates k = 4.12 vs. McAdams 6.78 W m−2 K−1/(m/s).
  • Calibrated physics beats persistence after only 100 real samples (perfect weather).
  • A Faiman model fit to the same data gives 3.96 K (steady state).

Closed-loop sandbox

The sandbox simulates the planned data acquisition system (Raspberry Pi Pico, 5 thermocouples, weather meter) with sensor errors and dropped packets.

  • MPC / oracle energy: 0.987
  • Fixed tilt / oracle energy: 0.678 (overstated, since the sandbox uses beam-only irradiance with no diffuse light)

Next Steps

  • Collect thermocouple data on our own panel in Clarksville, TN.
  • Refit k and C on that data, then regenerate the training set and retrain.
  • Model pose-dependent convection (tilt vs. wind direction), the one way wind could move the pose, and add diffuse light.

Project History

This project started in November 2025 as AURA-MF, a multi-fidelity Fortran simulator coupling the Boltzmann Transport Equation with Navier-Stokes. It was later narrowed to a single RK4 thermal generator, a neural surrogate, and the MPC described above. An earlier version of the work was presented at the Tennessee Academy of Science in April 2026.
