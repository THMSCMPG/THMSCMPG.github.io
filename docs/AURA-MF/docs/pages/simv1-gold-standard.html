Executive Summary

SimV1 is the high-fidelity gold standard for all AURA-MFP multi-fidelity simulations. It implements the Faiman thermal model with a transient panel energy-balance ODE, Monte Carlo photon transport for spectral mismatch (Beer-Lambert attenuation through the Si absorber), a Crank-Nicolson implicit heat solver, and Navier-Stokes boundary-layer closure for the convective coefficient. SimV1 generates the ground-truth training data for SimV2 and SimV3 and serves as the validation benchmark for SimV4. Target accuracy: RMSE < 1.0 K against the Sandia PVMC dataset.

Physical Modeling Framework

Faiman Thermal Model

SimV1 computes the convective heat transfer coefficient using the Faiman model:

$$h_{\text{conv}} = U_0 + U_1 \cdot u_\infty$$

where:

  • $U_0 \approx 25$ W m$^{-2}$ K$^{-1}$ — natural convection baseline
  • $U_1 \approx 6.84$ W m$^{-2}$ K$^{-1}$ (s m$^{-1}$) — wind enhancement coefficient
  • $u_\infty$ — wind speed [m s$^{-1}$]

Radiative Transport: MC Photon Transport for Spectral Mismatch

SimV1 uses Monte Carlo photon transport to compute the spectral mismatch between the incident spectrum and the AM1.5 reference spectrum via Beer-Lambert attenuation through the silicon absorber layer. The spectral absorption follows:

$$ \Phi(\lambda) = \Phi_0(\lambda) \cdot \exp\!\left(-\alpha(\lambda) \cdot d\right) $$

where $\alpha(\lambda)$ is the wavelength-dependent absorption coefficient of silicon and $d$ is the absorber thickness. The simulation spans $\lambda \in [280, 4000]$ nm using 120 wavelength bins.

Conjugate Heat Transfer Interface

The fluid-solid coupling at the PV panel surface $\Gamma$ requires simultaneous satisfaction of thermal equilibrium and heat flux continuity:

$$ \begin{cases} T_{\text{fluid}}\big|_{\Gamma} = T_{\text{solid}}\big|_{\Gamma} = T_{\Gamma} & \text{(Temperature continuity)} \\[8pt] -k_{\text{solid}} \frac{\partial T_{\text{solid}}}{\partial n}\bigg|_{\Gamma} = -k_{\text{fluid}} \frac{\partial T_{\text{fluid}}}{\partial n}\bigg|_{\Gamma} & \text{(Heat flux continuity)} \end{cases} $$

Transient Energy Balance ODE

Crank-Nicolson Heat Solver

SimV1 solves the full panel energy balance implicitly using Crank-Nicolson:

$$m c_p \frac{dT}{dt} = (\alpha - \eta_{\text{ref}}) G_{\text{poa}} A - h_{\text{conv}} A (T - T_{\text{amb}}) - \varepsilon \sigma A (T^4 - T_{\text{sky}}^4)$$

Solved over timesteps $\tau \in \{3600,\, 7200,\, 21600\}$ s.

  • $m c_p$ — panel thermal mass [J K$^{-1}$]
  • $\alpha$ — solar absorptance
  • $\eta_{\text{ref}}$ — reference electrical efficiency
  • $G_{\text{poa}}$ — plane-of-array irradiance [W m$^{-2}$]
  • $\varepsilon$ — panel thermal emissivity
  • $T_{\text{sky}}$ — effective sky temperature [K]

Navier-Stokes Boundary-Layer Closure

Convective Coefficient via Boundary-Layer Theory

The atmospheric boundary layer dynamics are governed by the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with Boussinesq approximation:

$$ \begin{aligned} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} &= 0 \\[8pt] \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} &= -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} + \beta g (T - T_\infty) \hat{\mathbf{z}} \end{aligned} $$

SimV1 uses the NS boundary-layer closure to compute the local convective coefficient from boundary-layer theory without resolving the full turbulent flow field. The Faiman $h_{\text{conv}}$ provides the calibrated output of this closure.

Adjoint Gradient Computation

Analytic Adjoint Sensitivities

SimV1 computes analytic adjoint sensitivities cross-checked against central differences. The gradients of the Sandia-derated efficiency $Y$ with respect to key model parameters are:

$$\frac{\partial Y}{\partial \alpha},\quad \frac{\partial Y}{\partial h_{\text{conv}}},\quad \frac{\partial Y}{\partial \varepsilon},\quad \frac{\partial Y}{\partial \eta_{\text{ref}}}$$

These gradients are used for parameter identification and to guide training data generation for SimV2/V3.

Current-Voltage Characteristics

Shockley-Queisser Single-Diode Model

SimV1 attempts to predict electrical performance using the modified diode equation with series ($R_s$) and shunt ($R_{sh}$) resistances:

$$ I = I_L - I_0 \left[\exp\left(\frac{V + IR_s}{n_{\text{id}} V_T}\right) - 1\right] - \frac{V + IR_s}{R_{sh}} $$

where:

  • $I_L = \int_{280}^{1100} \Phi(\lambda) \eta_{\text{IQE}}(\lambda) \, d\lambda$ is the photogenerated current
  • $I_0 = I_{0,\text{ref}} \left(\frac{T}{T_{\text{ref}}}\right)^3 \exp\left[-\frac{E_g}{k_B}\left(\frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_{\text{ref}}}\right)\right]$ is the dark saturation current
  • $V_T = k_B T / q$ is the thermal voltage
  • $n_{\text{id}} \approx 1.3$ is the ideality factor for polycrystalline silicon

Temperature Coefficient

The open-circuit voltage degrades linearly with temperature:

$$ \frac{dV_{oc}}{dT} = \frac{V_{oc}}{T} - \frac{E_g}{qT} + \frac{k_B}{q} \ln\left(\frac{I_{sc}}{I_0}\right) \cdot \frac{1}{T} $$

For typical c-Si modules, this yields $\beta_{V_{oc}} \approx -0.35\%$ per °C, consistent with manufacturer datasheets.

Validation Results

Sandia PVMC Benchmark

SimV1 will be validated against outdoor measurements from the Photovoltaic Module Characterization (PVMC) facility in Albuquerque, New Mexico (1619 m elevation, 35.05°N, 106.54°W).

Test Conditions:

  • Incident irradiance: $G = 1100 \pm 50$ W m$^{-2}$ (pyranometer-measured)
  • Ambient temperature: $T_\infty = 298.15 \pm 2$ K
  • Wind speed: $u_\infty = 1.0 \pm 0.5$ m s$^{-1}$
  • Relative humidity: RH $= 12 \pm 5\%$
