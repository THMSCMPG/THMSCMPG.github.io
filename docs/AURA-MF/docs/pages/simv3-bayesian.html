--- layout: AURA-MF title: SimV3 — Sandia Model with Energy Balance ---

Executive Summary

SimV3 extends the Sandia empirical model (SimV2) by adding an explicit transient energy-balance equation to track panel thermal mass dynamics during irradiance transients. SimV3b further adds the Fuentes thermal correction for full thermal mass effects. Together they provide the highest-accuracy empirical tier before the PINN-guided SimV4, and are the recommended tiers for high-accuracy thermal tracking applications.

Energy Balance Extension

Transient Panel Energy Balance

Unlike SimV2 which assumes instantaneous thermal equilibrium, SimV3 adds the panel thermal capacitance term to track the temperature trajectory during irradiance transients (cloud events, morning warm-up):

$$m c_p \frac{dT_{\text{panel}}}{dt} = G_{\text{poa}} (1 - \eta_{\text{cell}}) A - h_{\text{conv}} A (T_{\text{panel}} - T_{\text{amb}}) - \varepsilon \sigma A (T_{\text{panel}}^4 - T_{\text{sky}}^4)$$

where:

  • $m c_p$ — panel thermal mass [J K$^{-1}$]
  • $\eta_{\text{cell}}$ — cell electrical efficiency (temperature-dependent)
  • $h_{\text{conv}}$ — convective coefficient (from Sandia/Faiman model) [W m$^{-2}$ K$^{-1}$]
  • $\varepsilon$ — thermal emissivity of the panel surface
  • $T_{\text{sky}}$ — effective sky temperature [K]

Fuentes Thermal Correction (SimV3b)

Panel-Specific Thermal Mass

SimV3b implements the Fuentes (1987) thermal model, which explicitly accounts for the panel heat capacity $c_p$ [J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$] and effective thermal mass $m$ [kg m$^{-2}$]:

$$T_{\text{panel}}(t+\Delta t) = T_{\text{panel}}(t) + \frac{\Delta t}{m c_p} \left[ G_{\text{poa}} (\alpha - \eta) - h_{\text{conv}}(T_{\text{panel}} - T_{\text{amb}}) - \varepsilon \sigma (T_{\text{panel}}^4 - T_{\text{sky}}^4) \right]$$

This explicit Euler step provides an accurate discretisation of the thermal response for panels with significant thermal inertia (thick glass, aluminium frames).

Sky Temperature Model

Swinbank (1963) Formula

Both SimV3 and SimV3b use the Swinbank (1963) formula to estimate the effective sky temperature for the longwave radiation term:

$$T_{\text{sky}} = 0.0552 \cdot T_{\text{amb}}^{1.5}$$

(with $T_{\text{amb}}$ in Kelvin). This provides a simple but effective estimate of downwelling longwave radiation for clear-sky conditions.

Role in Fidelity Ladder

simv2 Sandia empirical (steady-state) No Broad range, fast evaluation

SimV3/3b are the recommended tiers for high-accuracy thermal tracking before resorting to the computationally intensive SimV1 or the PINN-routed SimV4.
